> **Principal finding.** On 7 October 2026 the company disclosed that the High Court of Malaya has made a charging order absolute over the 100,465 Tranglo shares held by its subsidiary Seamless Group, enforcing a Singapore judgment in favour of Ripple Markets APAC with approximately US$23.95M of principal unsatisfied. # 01 — Executive Summary

Currenc is a Cayman-incorporated foreign private issuer that reached Nasdaq in 2024 through the INFINT SPAC, in a de-SPAC from which approximately 98% of the trust was redeemed. It carries a going-concern qualification in two consecutive audited years, an accumulated deficit of US$150M, a declining operating business, and a capital structure that has expanded the share count by roughly 47% in ten months through an equity line, convertible notes and a resale registration equal to about 65% of the then-outstanding shares.

Its restructuring rested on two pillars. The first, a reverse merger with Animoca Brands, was suspended on 22 September 2026 with no new timetable. The second, the US$400M sale of its 60% interest in Tranglo, has not been announced as closed and now faces a charging order absolute over the very shares to be sold. Both pillars are impaired at the same time.

This report does not adopt a price target. A target of zero is not supported: the company has real revenue, a contracted US$400M disposal and a Chinese private-equity counterparty. The case is one of severe balance-sheet, enforcement and dilution risk, not of a worthless shell.

# 02 — The Tranglo Shares Are Encumbered and Subject to Forced Sale

By Form 6-K filed 7 October 2026, the company disclosed that it was informed on 1 October 2026 that the High Court of Malaya had made a charging order absolute over 100,465 ordinary shares in Tranglo Sdn. Bhd. held by Seamless Group Inc. The order enforces a Singapore judgment dated 17 April 2025 in favour of Ripple Markets APAC. Reported principal outstanding is approximately US$23.95M; some coverage frames the wider dispute at about US$27M.

From 7 October 2026 the judgment creditor may commence further enforcement and seek a sale of the charged shares. Seamless has indicated it may seek to protect its interests as to valuation, manner and terms of any sale.

> **Why this is the central fact.** The 100,465 shares under the charging order are the same 100,465 shares that are the subject of the January 2026 share purchase agreement with New Margin Holding. The company cannot deliver clean title to an asset that is charged in favour of a judgment creditor without first satisfying or discharging that judgment. This is the most plausible explanation on the public record for why a transaction agreed in January, with a long-stop of 30 September 2026, has not been announced as completed. It also means a creditor owed roughly US$24M may be able to force the sale of an asset the company values at US$400M, in a process the company does not control.

# 03 — Going Concern, Two Years Running

The FY2025 Form 20-F carries an auditor's going-concern paragraph. It records an accumulated deficit of US$150M and a net loss of US$18.5M for the year, and states that these conditions raise substantial doubt about the company's ability to continue as a going concern, with no adjustments made to the financial statements for the outcome of those uncertainties. The 20-F states that the audited statements for both FY2024 and FY2025 carry substantial-doubt disclosure.

The FY2024 position, from the F-1/A going-concern note as at 31 December 2024, was cash of US$63.8M against a working capital deficit of US$57.9M and a net capital deficit of US$41.8M, with a net loss of US$38.8M for the year.

# 04 — The Disposal Counterparty Is Also the Lender

New Margin Holding Limited is the offshore vehicle of NewMargin Ventures, a Chinese private equity firm described as managing more than RMB 40bn and having backed over 300 companies. It is the agreed buyer of the Tranglo stake for US$400M, payable US$200M at closing and US$200M within ninety days after closing.

The same counterparty is also the company's convertible note financier. On 8 October 2025 Currenc entered a Securities Purchase Agreement with New Margin for up to US$33M of unsecured convertible notes with warrants. An initial US$4.4M principal closed for approximately US$4M net, with the balance drawable in tranches subject to conditions.

> **Why the entanglement matters.** A single counterparty that is simultaneously (i) the acquirer of the issuer's principal operating asset, (ii) its convertible creditor and (iii) a prospective holder of its equity on conversion, has interests that are not aligned with public shareholders on price. The disposal price, the drawdown conditions and the conversion mechanics are negotiated across the same table. A buyer who is also a lender has leverage over a seller with a going-concern qualification and a judgment creditor at the door. The deferred US$200M second instalment is unsecured counterparty credit, not cash in hand.

## The instruments driving it

- **Arena equity line.** Entered 10 February 2025, initially up to US$10M of ordinary shares issuable in tranches. Filings list 10,000,000 shares plus 150,000 commitment-fee shares; a later prospectus describes up to 20,000,000 shares issuable to Arena plus 600,000 commitment-fee shares. An equity line issues shares at the issuer's election into whatever liquidity exists, which is structurally dilutive in a falling market.
- **Resale registration.** A Form F-1 (333-284957, 4 August 2025) registers the resale of up to 50,070,187 ordinary shares — approximately 65% of the 31 December 2025 share count — comprising the Arena shares and shares issued to creditors and noteholders. Prospectus Supplement No. 9 is dated 19 May 2026; a further 424B3 was filed 24 September 2026.
- **New Margin convertible notes.** Up to US$33M, maturing 8 October 2027, 5% interest, convertible at the holder's option at an initial US$1.85, with 50% warrant coverage also at US$1.85. Prepayment carries a 10% premium; on a change of control holders may demand 110% of principal or convert beforehand.
- **Ratchet warrants.** Warrants from the de-SPAC PIPE carry price protection for offerings at or above US$2.00 and full protection on both price and quantity for offerings below US$2.00. A full ratchet of that kind is the structural feature that converts a falling share price into accelerating dilution.

> **The threshold that matters.** The stock closed at US$2.42. The ratchet trigger is US$2.00 — about 17% below. The 52-week low is US$1.32, well beneath it. The convertible notes and their warrants strike at US$1.85, currently in the money for the holder. Any financing priced below US$2.00 re-prices and increases the warrant position. This is the mechanism by which a weak tape becomes permanent dilution, and it is the single most quantifiable downside pathway on the public record.

# 06 — The De-SPAC Left Almost Nothing Behind

Currenc reached Nasdaq via INFINT Acquisition Corporation. In connection with the special meeting, holders of 4,652,105 public shares redeemed at US$11.79 for approximately US$54.85M. An earlier redemption had already seen 2,661,404 Class A shares redeemed at about US$11.36, leaving roughly US$53.97M in trust as at 16 February 2024.

The trust balance immediately before closing was approximately US$1,119,024.

In other words, the company listed with effectively none of the SPAC's capital. The PIPE was correspondingly small: a convertible note of US$1,944,444 principal issued for US$1,750,000, plus 400,000 commitment shares and warrants over 136,110 shares, converting at US$10.00 and converted in full on 4 March 2025. A business that arrives on a public market with US$1.1M of trust money and a US$1.75M PIPE is dependent on post-listing financing from day one, which is precisely the pattern the subsequent equity line and convertible notes describe.

> **How to use this.** Prior litigation involving a chief executive is a governance datapoint, not proof of anything, and pleaded allegations must be described as pleaded. The relevant and verifiable points are narrower: the same individual led the 2018 acquisition of the Tranglo stake, was a defendant in litigation brought by that company's minority holders, and is now chief executive of the listed entity selling that same stake while it is charged to a judgment creditor. The continuity is the finding.

# 08 — Nasdaq Compliance

Nasdaq's Listing Qualifications staff issued a deficiency letter dated 12 January 2026 for failure to hold an annual general meeting within twelve months of fiscal year end, contrary to Listing Rules 5620(a) and 5810(c)(2)(G) and IM-5620. The notice had no immediate effect on listing. The company had 45 days to submit a compliance plan, with a potential extension of up to 180 days from fiscal year end, which the filings put at 29 June 2026. An extraordinary general meeting was scheduled for 25 February 2026.

The outcome was not established in this review. Note also that the share price and market capitalisation are not currently in breach of the minimum bid price or market value thresholds — see §12.

# 09 — The Animoca Suspension and the Audit Question

The transaction was announced on 3 November 2025: an acquisition of Animoca Brands by Australian scheme of arrangement under which Animoca shareholders would hold approximately 95% of the combined company. On 22 September 2026 the parties announced a suspension after exclusivity lapsed without a definitive agreement, citing a timetable that no longer fitted short- and medium-term objectives. Animoca states it remains committed to relisting; talks may resume if conditions permit.

One contemporaneous report attributes the suspension to audits remaining unfinished. If that is accurate it is a materially different and more adverse explanation than scheduling. It is recorded here as reported, not as established.

# 10 — Operating Deterioration Beneath the Headlines

- Revenue fell 18.5% to US$37.8M in FY2025 from US$46.4M; 10.3% excluding divested units.
- Global airtime revenue fell 14.9%; local airtime revenue fell 47.1%.
- Remittance revenue rose 22% to US$22.2M on higher volume — but Tranglo's take rate was flat at 0.37%, so the growth is volume at an unchanged, very thin margin. That is the segment being sold.
- Group EBITDA was a US$13.2M loss; Tranglo and WalletKu together contributed US$2.6M of positive EBITDA. Removing Tranglo removes the larger part of what works.
- Headquarters expense of US$16.5M against group revenue of US$37.8M — 44 cents of head-office cost for every revenue dollar.
